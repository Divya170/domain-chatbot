## Overview
- General, non-diagnostic information about check-ups during pregnancy from government guidelines. This is not medical advice. Always follow your doctor, ANM or health worker.
- Every pregnancy needs care: any pregnant woman can develop complications with little warning, so regular check-ups help detect problems early (PMSMA guideline).

## Benefits
- Number of check-ups:
  - SUMAN guarantees at least four antenatal check-ups: one in the first trimester, and at least one under PMSMA in the 2nd or 3rd trimester.
  - The PMSMA guideline describes "full ANC" as at least 3 ANC visits, 100 IFA (iron folic acid) tablets and 2 tetanus toxoid (TT) injections.
  - PMSMA gives a free check-up by a doctor on the 9th of every month for women in the 2nd and 3rd trimester.
- What is checked (government ANC standards):
  - Gestational age, history, general examination, weight at every visit, abdominal examination and fetal heart rate.
  - Screening for anaemia, high blood pressure (hypertensive disorders), diabetes, HIV, urine infection (bacteriuria), syphilis and malaria.
  - Blood group and Rh type at the first ANC visit.
  - One ultrasound is recommended during the 2nd/3rd trimester (PMSMA).
- Preventive care: care to prevent anaemia, neonatal tetanus, pre-eclampsia/eclampsia and malaria.
- All ANC tests, medicines and check-ups at government facilities are free under JSSK / SUMAN.
- Counselling topics during ANC: care during pregnancy, danger signs, birth preparedness and complication readiness, nutrition (iron folic acid and calcium), rest, safe sex, institutional delivery, referral transport, family planning, postnatal care, breastfeeding.
- MCP card: the Mother and Child Protection card records every check-up. Under PMSMA a coloured sticker shows risk status (green: no risk; red: high risk; blue: pregnancy-induced hypertension; yellow: diabetes, hypothyroidism, STIs or other co-morbid conditions).

## Notes
- A detailed list of danger signs is not given in the supplied documents. For heavy bleeding, severe pain, fits, high fever, severe headache or blurred vision, or if the baby stops moving, go to the nearest hospital or call an ambulance (102/108) immediately.
- The recommended timing of each visit (weeks of pregnancy) is not given in the supplied documents, except a PV examination at the 4th ANC visit (37 weeks or more). Ask your ANM or doctor for your schedule; the provider should share a written ANC schedule.
