## Overview
- JSSK was launched on 1 June 2011 (from Mewat district, Haryana) by the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare.
- It entitles all pregnant women delivering in public (government) health institutions to completely free, no-expense delivery, including caesarean section.
- Sick newborns get similar free entitlements in public health institutions up to 30 days after birth.
- The goal is nil out-of-pocket expense for pregnant women and sick newborns.
- JSSK is in addition to the JSY cash assistance.

## Eligibility
- All pregnant women accessing public (government) health institutions, for delivery including caesarean section.
- All sick newborns accessing public health institutions, up to 30 days after birth.

## Benefits
- For pregnant women:
  - Free delivery and free caesarean section.
  - Free drugs and consumables, including supplements such as Iron Folic Acid, during ANC, delivery and postnatal care up to 6 weeks, including management of complications.
  - Free essential diagnostics (blood tests, urine tests, ultrasonography, etc.) during ANC, delivery and postnatal care up to 6 weeks.
  - Free diet during the stay: up to 3 days for normal delivery and up to 7 days for caesarean section.
  - Free blood when required. Relatives are encouraged, but not required, to donate blood for replacement.
  - Free transport from home to the health institution.
  - Free transport between facilities if referred.
  - Free drop back from the institution to home after a 48-hour stay.
  - Exemption from all kinds of user charges (OPD, admission, tests, blood, etc.).
- For sick newborns up to 30 days after birth:
  - Free and zero-expense treatment.
  - Free drugs and consumables, free diagnostics and free blood.
  - Free transport from home, between facilities on referral, and drop back home.
  - Exemption from all kinds of user charges.

## How to apply
1. No application form or registration is needed. Go to any government health facility for ANC, delivery or newborn care.
2. For free transport, use the referral transport / ambulance number of your State (see the contacts file: 102 / 108).
3. If you are asked to pay or to buy medicines outside, use the grievance mechanism below.

## Official contacts
- Each government health facility should display, in public view, the names and phone numbers of grievance redressal authorities at facility, district and state level.
- Help desks and suggestion/complaint boxes should be available at government health facilities.

## Notes
- The guideline says facilities should keep fixed hours (at least 1 hour on any two working days a week) to meet complainants about free entitlements, and act on grievances within a suitable timeframe.
- The source is the 2011 guideline. Later expansions of JSSK (for example to antenatal/postnatal complications or to sick infants beyond 30 days) are not in the supplied documents and are unconfirmed. SUMAN (2019) also lists cashless services for sick newborns and infants; see the SUMAN file.
